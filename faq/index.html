In brief

Common questions, answered

A new piano should be tuned two to four times in its first year as the strings settle. After that, twice a year is a good rhythm for most homes. Pianos in dry or humid climates, or those used for teaching, may need more frequent tuning.

A weighted digital piano is the best start for most beginners. It costs less, needs no tuning, fits small spaces and lets you practise with headphones. Choose an acoustic upright when you want a traditional touch and have a dedicated room and a long term budget.

With short daily practice, most players can play a simple two hand song within four to eight weeks. Reaching a confident intermediate level usually takes one to three years. Consistency matters far more than long occasional sessions.

Many children start formal lessons between four and six, once they can focus for a short session and recognise left from right. Adults can start at any age and often progress quickly because they understand practice and choose their own repertoire.

A teacher gives the fastest reliable progress and catches technique problems early. If that is not in the budget, structured method books plus video lessons work well. Either way, regular feedback on what you play is the key ingredient.

The 80/20 rule means roughly 80 percent of your progress comes from 20 percent of your practice. Rather than playing a piece from the top again and again, isolate the few hard passages and repeat them slowly and correctly until they become easy.
