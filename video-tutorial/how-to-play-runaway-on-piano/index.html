How to Play Runaway on Piano

By Alice Baker
▶Video tutorial coming soon

Learn how to play Runaway on piano with a simple right-hand melody, easy left hand chords and a steady rhythm. Follow clear steps and play along at your own pace.

What you will learn

  1. Find the key centre first. Most easy arrangements of Runaway sit around A minor or C major, so start with your right thumb on A and get comfortable with the shape of the white keys before you learn any notes.
  2. Learn the recurring right hand motif in two bar chunks. Play the first phrase slowly, note by note, and say each note name aloud until the pattern feels automatic, then move to the next phrase.
  3. Add a simple left hand root note on the first beat of every bar: A, F, C and G. Keep it low, steady and relaxed so it supports the melody without overpowering it.
  4. Turn those roots into basic chords once the notes are secure: A minor, F major, C major and G major. Play them together with the right hand melody at a slow tempo.
  5. Practise hands separately before putting them together. Use a metronome set slower than you think you need, and only increase the speed when three clean repeats in a row feel easy.
  6. Shape the dynamics. Play the quiet verses softly and let the chorus open up, using the weight of your arm rather than pressing harder with your fingers.
  7. Practise the transitions between sections, especially the move from verse to chorus. Loop just the two bars around each change until the hands know where to go without thinking.
  8. Play the whole arrangement from start to finish once a day. Record yourself to catch uneven notes, then fix the hardest bar first rather than replaying the whole piece.
Tip Practise one step at a time and only move on when the current one feels easy.
