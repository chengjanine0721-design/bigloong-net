How Much Does a Piano Cost in 2026? (Complete Price Guide by Type)

By Alice Baker · 12 min read

A piano can cost anywhere from $200 to more than $80,000. The price you pay depends on the type, the brand, the condition and where you buy. This guide gives you real price ranges for digital pianos, upright pianos and grand pianos so you can match an instrument to your budget with confidence.

Most people asking how much does a piano cost want a single number. The honest answer is that there is no single number. A portable keyboard for a first lesson and a concert grand for a recording studio sit at opposite ends of a very wide market. Below you will find a quick price summary first, then a detailed breakdown of each category, brand pricing, the real costs for beginners and the hidden expenses that catch new buyers by surprise.

Quick answer Entry level digital pianos start around $200. A solid beginner upright runs $3,000 to $8,000. A baby grand usually starts near $12,000, and a premium concert grand can reach $80,000 or more.

How Much Does a Piano Cost? Price Ranges by Type

The single biggest factor in piano pricing is the type of instrument. Digital pianos are the most affordable and the most portable. Acoustic uprights cost more but need no power and offer a traditional touch. Grand pianos sit at the top of the range in both price and sound quality. Use this table as a fast reference before reading the detail.

Digital Pianos & Keyboards ($200 to $2,300+)

A digital piano uses sampled piano sounds and weighted or semi weighted keys to reproduce an acoustic instrument at a fraction of the size and cost. The digital piano cost you pay is driven mainly by key action quality, speaker power and the realism of the sound samples. They need no tuning, they stay in tune and most models include a headphone jack for quiet practice.

Entry Level Portable Keyboards ($400 to $450)

Portable keyboards in this band are light, easy to store and ideal if you are testing whether piano is right for you. Expect 61 to 88 keys with semi weighted action and built in speakers. They are a smart first step because the cost of a digital piano at this level is low enough to treat as a trial. If you are still deciding where to begin, our guide to starting piano as a beginner walks through the first weeks of practice.

Mid Range Console & Bundles ($650 to $750)

Step up to this band and you get 88 fully weighted keys, better sound engines, stronger speakers and often a stand and bench bundled in. A console digital piano looks more like a real piano and suits a living room where the instrument stays in one place. This is the sweet spot for many families.

High End Digital & Cabinet Pianos ($1,300 to $6,800)

Premium digital pianos use the most realistic hammer action and detailed concert grand samples. Cabinet models sit in a wooden frame that resembles an upright piano and fill a room with richer sound. At this level how much does a good piano cost becomes a question of how close you want to feel to a real acoustic instrument without the maintenance.

Acoustic Upright Pianos ($3,000 to $25,000)

An upright piano is the classic home instrument. Its strings sit vertically, which saves space and keeps the price below a grand. Because it is fully acoustic, it has a warm, natural tone and a touch that responds to how you play. This category directly answers how much does a piano cost upright, with prices shaped by size, age, brand and condition.

Entry Level & Student Uprights

New student uprights from reputable makers start around $3,000 and rise to roughly $7,000. These are built for daily practice and hold their value well. A well maintained used upright can cost far less, often $1,000 to $3,000, but you should budget for an inspection and possible servicing before you buy.

Professional & Studio Uprights

Taller uprights with longer strings and stronger frames deliver a fuller sound and a more responsive action. Prices commonly run $8,000 to $25,000. Teachers, advanced students and home studios favour these because they behave much like a small grand in feel and tone.

Baby Grand & Grand Pianos ($12,000 to $80,000+)

Grand pianos use a horizontal string layout and a gravity return action, which gives the player the finest control and the richest sound. They are the largest and most expensive category, and they need a dedicated space.

Baby Grand Pianos

A baby grand measures roughly five to six feet long and starts near $12,000, with most models selling between $15,000 and $30,000. It fits comfortably in a generous living room and delivers a genuine grand piano experience without the footprint of a full concert instrument.

Full Size & Concert Grands

Grand pianos of seven feet and above begin around $20,000 and climb quickly. Concert grands from seven to nine feet can reach $80,000 or more. At this level you are paying for projection, tonal complexity and the kind of action that professional pianists rely on.

Steinway & Luxury Grand Pianos

Steinway & Sons sits at the premium end of the market. A new Steinway grand commonly starts around $60,000 and can exceed $100,000 for a concert size model. If you are researching steinway piano cost or how much does a steinway grand piano cost, expect a hand built instrument whose price reflects decades of craft and strong resale value. Used Steinways offer a more accessible route into the brand.

Piano Prices by Brand: Yamaha, Kawai, Roland, Steinway

Brand has a clear effect on price and resale value. Some makers focus on acoustic instruments, some on digital pianos and some on both. The figures below are typical ranges for new instruments and give you a realistic picture of how much does a good piano cost from each name.

Yamaha Piano Prices

Yamaha is the most recognised piano brand in the world and builds both acoustic and digital instruments. Entry digital pianos begin near $500, uprights run roughly $4,000 to $20,000 and baby grands start around $18,000. If you are comparing how much does a piano cost yamaha against other brands, Yamaha usually sits in the middle to upper range with excellent reliability and strong resale value.

Kawai Piano Prices

Kawai is known for responsive actions and warm tone. Its digital pianos start around $600, uprights from $4,500 to $18,000 and grands from about $16,000. Kawai often competes directly with Yamaha at a similar price, so the choice usually comes down to touch preference.

Roland & Other Digital Brands

Roland leads the high end digital market. Home models start near $700 and flagship cabinet pianos can reach $6,000 or more. Nord, Casio and Korg cover the rest of the digital space, with Casio offering some of the best value entry keyboards and Nord focusing on stage ready instruments for performers.

Steinway & Premium Brands

Beyond Steinway, premium makers such as Bösendorfer, Fazioli and Mason & Hamlin build instruments priced from $40,000 well into six figures. These are specialist instruments chosen by concert pianists and collectors. For most buyers a mid range Yamaha, Kawai or Roland delivers everything needed at a fraction of the price.

How to Choose the Right Piano for Your Budget

Price is only one part of the decision. The right instrument depends on your space, your goals and how long you plan to keep playing. If you are unsure whether piano is a long term commitment, start with a digital instrument before you put a deposit on an acoustic piano.

Match Piano Type to Your Skill & Space

A renter in a small apartment is better served by a portable digital piano with headphones than a full upright. A family with a dedicated room and a committed student can justify an acoustic instrument that will last for decades. Think about noise, moving difficulty and available floor space before you compare price tags.

Budget Checklist

  • Set a firm ceiling that includes delivery and accessories, not just the instrument.
  • Decide whether portability or traditional touch matters more to you.
  • Compare new and used options, and price an inspection for any used acoustic piano.
  • Read our beginner piano guide if you are still choosing a first instrument.

How Much Is a Piano for Beginners?

New players do not need to spend a fortune. A capable first instrument can cost less than a phone, and you can upgrade later once your playing grows. The question how much is a piano for beginners really depends on how committed you are at the start.

Best Budget for First Piano ($100 to $500)

If you are testing the waters, a 61 key or entry 88 key digital piano in this band is enough to learn notes, chords and simple pieces. Look for weighted keys if your budget allows. Used keyboards and entry bundles appear regularly on the second hand market.

Best Value for New Players ($500 to $1,000)

This range buys a proper 88 key weighted digital piano with good speakers and a realistic feel. It is the most popular band for beginners because it balances cost and quality. Many players keep these instruments for years before considering an upgrade.

When to Invest More ($1,000+)

Once you play daily and can hear the difference in tone and action, spending more starts to pay off. A high end digital piano or an entry acoustic upright gives you a better touch and sound that supports faster progress. Invest when the instrument becomes part of your routine rather than a new hobby.

Hidden Costs of Buying a Piano

The sticker price is not the full cost of ownership. Acoustic pianos in particular need ongoing care and careful handling. Budget for the items below so there are no surprises after delivery.

Tuning & Regulation Fees

Acoustic pianos need tuning at least once or twice a year, typically costing $100 to $250 per visit. Regulation, which adjusts the action, is recommended every few years and can cost several hundred dollars. Digital pianos need none of this maintenance.

Delivery & Moving Costs

Moving an upright or grand piano requires specialist movers. Local delivery often runs $150 to $500, and a long distance move can cost much more. Stairs, narrow doors and long carries all add to the bill. This is also where how much does a piano weigh becomes relevant, since a typical upright weighs 200 to 300 pounds and a grand can exceed 600 pounds.

Accessories (Bench, Pedals, Stand)

Plan for a stable bench, a sustain pedal and a stand if they are not included. A decent bench costs $50 to $200, a quality pedal $30 to $150 and a stand $50 to $200. These add up, so check what is bundled before you compare final prices.

FAQ

The 80/20 rule means roughly 80 percent of your progress comes from 20 percent of your practice. In practice, spend most of your time on the few difficult bars that hold a piece back rather than playing it from the top again and again. Slow, focused repetition on the hard spots gives far better results than long unfocused sessions.

Playing the piano is an active form of music engagement that several studies link to reduced cortisol, the body's stress hormone. Practising and playing for pleasure can lower stress and improve mood, though the effect varies from person to person. Even short daily sessions are associated with a calmer state of mind.

Conclusion: Is Owning a Piano Worth It?

A piano is a long term investment in yourself. Whether you spend $300 on a first digital keyboard or $8,000 on an upright, the value comes from the hours you put into it. For most beginners the best choice is a well reviewed digital piano in the $500 to $1,000 band, which gives you a real weighted feel without a large commitment. As your playing develops, you can move to a better instrument with a clear sense of what you actually need.

Use the ranges in this guide to set a realistic budget, include delivery and accessories in that budget, and choose the type that matches your space and goals. With the right instrument, the question of how much does a piano cost stops being about price and starts being about how much music you will make with it.
